The system is powered from an external \SI{12}{V} power supply connected through a barrel jack connector.
The \SI{12}{V} powers the stepper motor driver and is simultaneously reduced to \SI{5}{V} using a buck converter.
The \SI{5}{V} output is used to power the Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W and the \gls{lidar} sensor.

The platform is rotated using a stepper motor mentioned in \cref{subsec:controlling_the_platform}, which is controlled through the stepper motor driver. 
The driver receives control signals from the microcontroller via STEP and DIR pins. 
A \SI{100}{\micro\farad} capacitor is connected across the driver's supply terminals in order to suppress voltage spikes generated during motor switching.
RESET and SLEEP pins of the driver are tied together and connected to \SI{5}{V} to keep the driver enabled at all times.
A microstepping mode of 2:1 is set by connecting the MODE0 pin to \SI{5}{V} and leaving MODE1 and MODE2 pins unconnected \cite[sec.~8.3.5]{alldatasheet.com_DRV8825Download_}.

The \gls{lidar} sensor communicates with the microcontroller via the \gls{uart} interface, using the RX pin of the microcontroller. The acquired data are partially parsed by the Pico and subsequently sent to a PC via Wi-Fi.

Additionally, a Hall effect sensor module is connected to the microcontroller and is used for the homing mechanism of the platform.
This initialisation sequence is performed before each scan in order to prevent twisting of the wires connecting the \gls{lidar} sensor to the microcontroller.
